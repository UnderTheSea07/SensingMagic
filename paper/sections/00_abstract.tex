\section*{Abstract}

Robotic and wearable systems require compliant tactile interfaces that resolve
weak, rapidly varying and spatially distributed mechanical stimuli. Yet no
existing tactile skin delivers weak-force sensitivity, multi-axis perception and
large-area coverage without prohibitive readout complexity. Here we report a
magnetic cilia tactile skin that pairs a dense passive layer of magnetized
elastomer cilia with distributed tri-axis magnetic readout. Geometry
optimization across six cilia designs establishes \dataneeded{optimal length
$\times$ diameter, e.g.\ $L$\,=\,x\,mm, $d$\,=\,x\,mm} and yields a sensitivity
enhancement of \dataneeded{gain $G_{\mathrm{cilia}}$ with 95\% CI, $n \geq 3$
patches}-fold over a matched flat magnetic control. A local $2\times2$ sensing
unit reconstructs unseen contact positions with a localization error of
\dataneeded{RMSE in mm, unseen-position test set} and estimates normal force to
within \dataneeded{NRMSE, \%}. Integrated into a 44-node hand-shaped array, the
skin generates distributed tactile maps at \dataneeded{measured full-frame rate,
Hz} while suppressing motion-induced magnetic artifacts by \dataneeded{CMR, dB}.
The same platform resolves airflow between \dataneeded{min wind speed,
m\,s$^{-1}$} and \dataneeded{max wind speed, m\,s$^{-1}$} and records
radial-artery pulse micro-vibrations synchronized with electrocardiography
across \dataneeded{number of subjects}. Magnetic cilia thus provide a scalable,
wire-free transduction layer that unifies robotic touch, environmental sensing
and physiological monitoring within one compliant skin.
