\section*{Introduction}

Embodied intelligence, whether in robotic manipulators or on-body wearable
interfaces, depends on tactile sensing that is simultaneously large-area,
mechanically compliant, multi-axis, dynamic and sensitive to weak forces
\cite{dahiya2010tactile,chortos2016pursuing}. Dexterous manipulation and
safe human contact require resolving not only firm grasping loads but also
light touch, shear, incipient slip, weak airflow and even physiological
micro-vibrations such as the pulse \cite{sundaram2019learning}.
Yet most tactile platforms are optimized for a narrow slice of this space, and
no single skin resolves subtle, spatially distributed and rapidly varying
mechanical stimuli across an entire hand.

Existing approaches each face structural trade-offs. Capacitive and
piezoresistive skins scale to large areas but suffer from dense interconnect
routing, cross-talk, baseline drift and limited weak-force resolution
\cite{sundaram2019learning,boutry2018hierarchically}. Piezoelectric and
triboelectric devices excel at dynamic transients but respond poorly to
sustained static loads \refneeded{piezoelectric tactile}. Vision-based tactile
sensors achieve high spatial resolution but require an internal camera,
illumination and per-contact computation, constraining thickness, curvature
and integration density \cite{yuan2017gelsight}. Conventional magnetic
skins offer contactless, drift-tolerant, multi-axis readout, but their planar
magnet-film geometries often limit weak-stimulus sensitivity, directional
decoupling or scalable coverage \cite{hellebrekers2019soft,yan2021soft}.

Biological systems resolve weak mechanical signals using slender,
high-aspect-ratio appendages---mammalian whiskers, insect sensilla and hair-cell
structures---that convert faint airflow, contact and vibration into amplified
mechanical deflection \cite{park2023bioinspired}. Magnetic cilia have been
explored for tactile and airflow transduction at the single-element or
small-array scale \cite{alfadhel2015magnetic,man2023tactile}; we translate this
principle into an ordered, full-hand array of magnetized elastomer cilia. Their high
aspect ratio makes them compliant to weak shear, flow and vibration; because
transduction is magnetic and read out by underlying tri-axis sensors, the cilia
require no individual wiring and act as a passive mechanical amplification layer
that is naturally compatible with sparse, distributed magnetic sampling.

Here we report a magnetic cilia tactile skin and validate it against five
questions: why cilia rather than a flat magnetic film; whether a local
$2\times2$ tri-axis unit can invert contact position, force and direction at
unseen locations; whether this local capability scales to a 44-node full-hand
array; whether the resulting whole-hand maps reflect true contact rather than
posture, geomagnetic or cable-motion artifacts; and whether the platform
captures weak dynamic disturbances---airflow and pulse---that rigid force
sensors miss. Our contributions are (i) a geometry-optimized magnetic cilia
transduction layer with a quantified enhancement over matched flat controls,
(ii) local $2\times2$ inverse decoding validated on unseen positions,
(iii) a 44-node full-hand array with explicit motion-artifact suppression and
trustworthy spatial mapping, and (iv) shared sensing of weak external airflow
and internal pulse micro-vibrations on one platform.

Together these results position magnetic cilia as a scalable mechanical
transduction layer for embodied perception, with implications for compliant
robotics, wearable tactile interfaces and the monitoring of subtle
environmental and physiological signals \refneeded{embodied sensing outlook}.
