\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Materials and magnetic particle preparation}
2000-mesh NdFeB powder (nominal particle size $\approx$6.5~$\mu$m;
\dataneeded{grade, supplier}) was combined with the elastomer matrix:
Ecoflex~00-30 for the tactile-skin cilia patches (compositions of
50--80~wt\% NdFeB were screened mechanically; devices use 60~wt\%
\dataneeded{confirm 60 vs 70 wt\% against the fabrication log}) and PDMS
(base:curing agent 10:1 by mass) for the geometry/reliability sample series
(\dataneeded{confirm matrix assignment per sample batch}). The effective
magnetization of the cured composite, calibrated by matching finite-element
tip-deflection case studies (deviation $<$0.1\%), is
$M\approx82$~kA\,m$^{-1}$.

\subsection*{Cilia mould design and fabrication}
Moulds (\dataneeded{material, machining/printing method, resolution}) defined
cilia of $L=3,5,8$~mm and $d=0.5,0.8,1.0$~mm; array samples used pitches of
0.5 or 1.0~mm (geometry-matrix sample pitch: \dataneeded{pitch $s$}).
Process (per the established workflow): mixing, vacuum degassing (15~min),
pre-cure (5~min at \dataneeded{temperature}), mould filling/casting, magnetic
pre-alignment under a uniform field, full cure (60~min at
\dataneeded{temperature}) and demoulding.

\subsection*{Magnetic pre-alignment and final magnetization}
The NdFeB-loaded elastomer was pre-aligned under a uniform magnetic field
(\dataneeded{field strength, direction}) applied between casting and full
cure; after demoulding, samples were magnetized to saturation in a strong
field (\dataneeded{magnetizer type, field strength, direction}).

\subsection*{Geometry measurement}
Height, diameter and pitch measured on \dataneeded{$n\geq 30$ cilia, $\geq 3$
patches} by \dataneeded{microscope/method}; report mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.

\subsection*{Mechanical testing}
Dogbone specimens were tested in uniaxial tension per DIN~EN~ISO~527-1 on a
ZwickRoell testing machine (\dataneeded{model, strain rate}). Engineering
stress was computed from the load and each specimen's measured cross-section
($A_0\approx1.1$--$2.3$~mm$^2$); the tensile modulus $E_t$ is the linear-fit
slope of engineering stress versus dimensionless strain over the 20--40\%
strain interval. Two specimen series were tested: (i)~magnetization-direction
groups (non-magnetized $n=5$, $x$-magnetized $n=2$, $y$-magnetized $n=4$,
$z$-magnetized $n=5$ valid specimens); and (ii)~a composition series of
Ecoflex~00-30 with 50/60/70/80~wt\% NdFeB
($n=$~5/4/4/5 specimens, respectively).

\subsection*{Magnetic characterization}
No-load field, sensitivity and LOD per geometry; report $\sigma_B$ and
$S=\mathrm{d}|\Delta\mathbf{B}|/\mathrm{d}F$.

\subsection*{Geometry--sensitivity characterization (completed dataset)}
Two complementary datasets were acquired with a single MLX90393 beneath the
cilia (magnetic logging at $\approx$1100~Hz) and an ATI six-axis force/torque
sensor (S/N FT35606; 1000~Hz acquisition with 16-sample averaging, 62.5~Hz
effective) as force reference, with loading applied manually at nominal
indentation depths of 1, 2 and 3~mm, normally and tangentially, in repeated
load--hold ($\sim$15--25~s)--unload events. (i)~\emph{Geometry matrix}: the
nine $(L,d)$ combinations were each measured with $\geq$3 trials per
condition point, yielding 44 valid sensitivity points (one artifact
excluded). (ii)~\emph{Cilia-array dataset}: square arrays of $2\times2$ to
$5\times5$ cilia at pitches of 0.5 or 1.0~mm ($L=3$ and 5~mm), read by a
sensor $\approx$1~mm beneath the array centre; 20 trials per condition, 1871
of 1920 trials passing quality control (97.4\%). Processing used Hampel
despiking, zero-phase Butterworth low-pass filtering (magnetic 20~Hz, force
10~Hz), pre-load baseline subtraction and loading-edge alignment of the two
streams (median alignment correlation $r=0.79$); the per-trial sensitivity is
the plateau ratio $S=|\Delta B|/F$. A dipole-superposition large-deflection
beam model of the cilium, validated against finite-element case studies
(tip-deflection deviation $<$0.1\%), reproduces measured single-cilium
responses with relative errors of 0.27--0.41\%. Because loading was manual,
per-trial depth varies (peak-amplitude CV 13.4\%); the robot-controlled
protocol below supersedes these datasets for force-calibrated claims.

\subsection*{Cyclic fatigue testing}
Three independent samples were cycled in compression to 150{,}000 cycles
under machine-driven periodic loading (period 0.512~s, $\approx$2.0~Hz;
drive amplitude 0.52--0.62~N; actuator: \dataneeded{model}), with
interleaved checkpoints at 5k--150k cycles ($\sim$1000 recorded cycles per
checkpoint; the 10k-checkpoint block additionally captures all 10{,}000
cycles continuously). Sensitivity per checkpoint
was computed as the ratio of median magnetic to median force peak-to-peak
amplitude ($S=\Delta B_z/\Delta F_z$), using ratio normalization to remove
drive-amplitude changes; checkpoints with $>$9\% corrupted-packet rate were
flagged low-confidence and displayed as open symbols.

\subsection*{xArm--Nano17 loading protocol}
Controlled loading used a UFactory~xArm (\dataneeded{model}) carrying an
ATI~Nano17 six-axis force/torque sensor (\dataneeded{calibration, resolution})
with a non-magnetic probe (\dataneeded{PEEK/nylon/acrylic/ceramic};
\dataneeded{tip geometry, e.g.\ 2~mm hemisphere}). Before each campaign we
verified that (i) the probe raised no magnetic signal above $3\sigma$ of the
sensor noise when hovered 1~mm above the skin without contact, (ii) the arm
executing a typical trajectory 5~cm above the sample produced motion-induced
$|\Delta\mathbf{B}|$ below $3\sigma$ (otherwise the standoff was increased and
the residual reported), and (iii) the Nano17 zero drifted less than
\dataneeded{mN} over 5~min. Each trial followed a fixed profile: pre-contact
baseline, approach at \dataneeded{approach speed, mm\,s$^{-1}$} to the target
normal force, a hold of \dataneeded{hold duration, s}, then retraction, with
force setpoints and, for shear, in-plane displacement commanded in closed loop
against the Nano17 reading.

\subsection*{Time synchronization}
The MLX90393 array (\dataneeded{ODR, Hz}), Nano17 (\dataneeded{rate, Hz}), xArm
pose stream and video were logged on a single host, each sample carrying a host
timestamp. Streams were hard-aligned per trial to a common event---a brief
probe tap (contact-to-release $<$100~ms) at trial onset that appears
simultaneously as a force spike and a magnetic transient---giving a residual
synchronization error of \dataneeded{ms} (verified by 10 repeated taps).

\subsection*{Baseline correction}
For every trial the per-channel baseline was the mean over a
\dataneeded{pre-contact window, s} pre-contact window and was subtracted to
give $\Delta\mathbf{B}$. Slow drift was monitored by returning the probe to a
fixed reference position every \dataneeded{$N$} trials (anchor trials); anchor
drift exceeding \dataneeded{threshold, $\mu$T} triggered re-baselining of the
intervening block. Trials logged as mechanically disturbed were excluded and
re-recorded.

\subsection*{Temperature compensation}
The MLX90393 on-die temperature channel was recorded continuously. A
per-channel affine temperature model $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{corr}}=\mathbf{B}-
\boldsymbol{\beta}(T-T_0)$ was fitted on no-load segments spanning the working
temperature range and applied before baseline subtraction
(\dataneeded{fitted $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ range}).

\subsection*{Signal filtering}
Distinct bands were used for the three regimes and reported separately:
quasi-static contact (\dataneeded{low-pass cutoff, Hz}), airflow-induced
fluctuation (band-pass \dataneeded{f1--f2, Hz}) and pulse (band-pass
\dataneeded{f1--f2, Hz}; a preliminary 0.5--10~Hz Butterworth was used for the
demonstration in Extended Data). All filters were zero-phase (forward--backward)
Butterworth of order \dataneeded{order}, implemented in SciPy
(\dataneeded{version}); filter type, order and cutoffs are stated with every
result.

\subsection*{Motion-artifact suppression}
A cilia-free reference MLX90393 placed away from the contact surface
(\dataneeded{location}) captured common-mode field variation from posture,
cables and ambient sources. Each sensing node was corrected as
$\Delta\mathbf{B}_{i,\mathrm{corr}}=\Delta\mathbf{B}_i-\alpha_i
\Delta\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ref}}$, with per-node gains $\alpha_i$ fitted by
least squares on no-contact motion recordings. Suppression was quantified by
the contact-to-motion ratio $\mathrm{CMR}=20\log_{10}(A_{\mathrm{contact}}/
A_{\mathrm{motion}})$ before and after correction, and by the false-positive
rate on held-out no-contact motions.

\subsection*{Inverse model}
The feature vector was the concatenated tri-axis reading of every node in a
unit (12 channels for the $2\times2$). Four model families were compared:
nearest-neighbour fingerprint matching, ridge/polynomial regression, random
forest and a compact multilayer perceptron. Targets were predicted in three
cumulative stages---$(x,y)$; $(x,y,F_n)$; $(x,y,F_n,F_x,F_y)$---so that added
outputs could not degrade earlier ones unnoticed. Hyperparameters were selected
on the validation split only (\dataneeded{search space and selected values});
training used \dataneeded{scikit-learn/PyTorch version}.

\subsection*{Train/validation/test split}
Splits were made \emph{by contact position}: of the grid positions,
\dataneeded{n} were assigned to training, \dataneeded{n} to validation and
\dataneeded{n} to an unseen-position test set (60/20/20), so that no test
position appeared during training. No time frame from a given trial was shared
across splits, preventing frame-level leakage. Generalization across
re-mounting was assessed by training and testing on different sessions
(\dataneeded{number of sessions}); the unseen-position test set was evaluated
once, at the end.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis}
Throughout, $n$ denotes independent devices, patches or subjects, reported
separately from technical repeats; figures show individual points with
mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.\ or 95\% confidence intervals. Group comparisons used
two-sided tests (\dataneeded{test names, e.g.\ ANOVA with Holm correction})
with exact $P$ values, effect sizes and \dataneeded{multiple-comparison
correction}; confidence intervals for the cilia gain $G_{\mathrm{cilia}}$ were
obtained by bootstrap over independent patches. Condition order was randomized
and the randomization seed stored with each dataset.

\subsection*{Wind-tunnel setup}
Airflow was generated by \dataneeded{tunnel/fan model} and measured with a
reference anemometer (\dataneeded{model, accuracy}). To separate cilia
aeroelastic response from fan-motor electromagnetic pickup, every speed was
also recorded with a bare (cilia-free) sensor and with the fan running but the
flow ducted away (blocked-duct sham). Recordings alternated flow-off and
flow-on intervals within each file so that a within-file baseline removes
drift. Speeds \dataneeded{levels, incl.\ $<$2~m\,s$^{-1}$} were presented in
randomized order with \dataneeded{runs, $\geq$5} independent runs each; the
2025-07-17 single-sensor session that lacked these controls is documented as
insufficient in the Extended Data.

\subsection*{Pulse experiment and ethics}
The study was approved by \dataneeded{IRB/committee, protocol number} and all
participants gave written informed consent. Radial-artery recordings from
$n=$~\dataneeded{10--20} subjects were acquired with hardware-synchronized ECG
(\dataneeded{device}) and, where available, PPG, the reference and magnetic
streams sharing a common trigger (alignment verified to \dataneeded{ms}). Each
subject completed \dataneeded{sessions} sessions with re-attachment between
sessions; patch location, attachment pressure, wrist side, posture and subject
ID were logged. Heart-rate accuracy, ECG-to-pulse delay, waveform correlation,
SNR and beat-to-beat variability were computed against the ECG reference;
morphological features were reported only where stable across beats and
subjects.
