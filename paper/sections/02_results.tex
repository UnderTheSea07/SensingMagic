\subsection*{System design for embodied perception of subtle mechanical stimuli}
% -> Fig. 1
The tactile skin integrates a passive magnetic cilia layer, a soft elastomer/
adhesive interface, a distributed MLX90393 tri-axis magnetic sensing layer and
a hand-shaped flexible printed circuit (Fig.~1). Cilia are confined to the
palmar side---palm, finger pads and fingertips---so that the sensing surface
matches the contact surface of a grasping hand (Fig.~1a). Weak mechanical
inputs deflect the cilia, producing distinct three-axis magnetic signatures
under normal versus shear loading that are sampled by the underlying sensors
(Fig.~1d). Sparse tri-axis sampling from a local $2\times2$ unit, a $1\times4$
strip and the full 44-node array is combined with calibrated spatial
reconstruction to move from local decoding to whole-hand mapping (Fig.~1e).
The integrated system comprises 44 MLX90393 nodes with
\dataneeded{number of effective/active nodes} active channels covering
\dataneeded{palmar coverage area, cm$^2$}, with a total thickness of
\dataneeded{system thickness, mm} and mass of \dataneeded{system mass, g}.
It streams a local $2\times2$ frame at \dataneeded{2x2 frame rate, Hz} and a
full 44-node frame at \dataneeded{full-hand frame rate, Hz}, with a dropped-frame
rate of \dataneeded{dropped-frame \% over 10 min} over 10~min of continuous

\subsection*{Geometry-dependent amplification and physical characterization of magnetic cilia}
% -> Fig. 2
We fabricated NdFeB--PDMS cilia by mixing, degassing, mould filling,
magnetic pre-alignment, curing and demoulding, followed by final magnetization
(Fig.~2a,b; Methods); the effective magnetization, calibrated against
finite-element case studies, is $M\approx82$~kA\,m$^{-1}$. Cilia were produced
across a geometry matrix of lengths $L=3,5,8$~mm and diameters
$d=0.5,0.8,1.0$~mm (nine combinations) at a pitch of
\dataneeded{measured pitch $s$, mm} (Fig.~2c). For each geometry we measured
\dataneeded{$n$ cilia measured per geometry, target $\geq 30$ from $\geq 3$
patches} cilia and report mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.\ of height, diameter and pitch.
Tensile characterization of non-magnetic and $x$/$y$/$z$-magnetized
specimens gives moduli of $0.72\pm0.11$ (non-magnetic, $n=5$),
$0.85\pm0.09$ ($x$, $n=2$), $0.73\pm0.11$ ($y$, $n=4$) and
$0.68\pm0.11$~MPa ($z$-magnetized, $n=5$; mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.); the
$x$-magnetized group (magnetization aligned with the tensile axis) shows a
trend towards higher stiffness, while the remaining groups are
indistinguishable within one standard deviation. Across the Ecoflex~00-30
composition series, raising the NdFeB content from 50 to 70~wt\% leaves the
modulus nearly unchanged ($0.130\pm0.012$ to $0.152\pm0.026$~MPa) whereas
80~wt\% stiffens the composite roughly sixfold ($0.836\pm0.095$~MPa) and
reduces elongation at break from above 400\% to $\approx$150\%, defining the
compliance--magnetic-loading trade-off available for cilia design.
Loading--unloading hysteresis is \dataneeded{mechanical hysteresis, \%}
(Fig.~2d).

Geometry-dependent sensitivity was mapped by loading each of the nine
geometries normally and tangentially at indentation depths of 1, 2 and 3~mm
against an ATI force/torque reference, extracting the plateau ratio
$S=|\Delta\mathbf{B}|/F$ per trial ($\geq 3$ trials per point; 44 valid
condition points, one excluded artifact). A second-order response surface over
$(L,d)$ captures the trend with $R^2=0.92$ for tangential and $R^2=0.68$ for
normal loading, with sensitivities spanning approximately 3--50~mT\,N$^{-1}$; the longest and
thickest cilia ($L=8$~mm, $d=1.0$~mm) give the largest median response
(tangential 45~mT\,N$^{-1}$), consistent with the larger magnetic moment per
cilium, and large-depth points fall systematically below the small-signal
trend, consistent with deflection saturation (Fig.~2e). Repeated manual loading on a fixed patch gave
a peak-amplitude coefficient of variation of 13.4\% across 20 trials,
dominated by depth variability of the manual protocol. The comparison against
matched controls---a flat magnetic film, short magnetic pillars, non-magnetic
cilia and a bare MLX90393---required for the cilia gain
$G_{\mathrm{cilia}}=S_{\mathrm{cilia}}/S_{\mathrm{flat}}$ and the limit of
detection $\mathrm{LOD}=3\sigma_B/S$ remains to be acquired:
\dataneeded{flat-film / pillar / non-magnetic / bare-sensor control data,
$G_{\mathrm{cilia}}$ with 95\% CI, LOD}. Dynamic characterization from high-rate tap events gives a
10--90\% response time of 25~ms and recovery of 26~ms for the fastest events
(median response 50~ms across 81 events); cyclic fatigue over 150{,}000
compression cycles on three independent samples shows no significant
sensitivity degradation (within $\pm$4\% from 50k to 150k cycles on the two
samples with reliable acquisition), and baseline drift over
\dataneeded{drift over $\geq$30~min} remains to be quantified (Fig.~2f).

\noindent\textit{Claim gate.} The term ``cilia-amplified'' is used only if the

$\geq 3$ independent devices and statistical significance, after controlling for
total magnetic material and no-load field; otherwise we use ``cilia-enhanced''
or ``magnetic cilia tactile skin'' (see notes/claims\_table.md).
