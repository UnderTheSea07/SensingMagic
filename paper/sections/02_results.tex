\subsection*{System design for embodied perception of subtle mechanical stimuli}
% -> Fig. 1
The tactile skin integrates four layers: a passive magnetic cilia layer, a
soft elastomer/adhesive interface, a distributed layer of MLX90393 tri-axis
magnetic sensors and a hand-shaped flexible printed circuit (Fig.~1). Cilia
are confined to the palmar side---palm, finger pads and fingertips---so that
the sensing surface coincides with the contact surface of a grasping hand
(Fig.~1a). Weak mechanical inputs deflect the cilia and imprint distinct
three-axis magnetic signatures under normal versus shear loading, which the
underlying sensors sample directly (Fig.~1d). Sensing proceeds
hierarchically: sparse tri-axis sampling from a local $2\times2$ unit, a
$1\times4$ strip and the full 44-node array is combined with calibrated
spatial reconstruction, carrying local decoding up to whole-hand mapping
(Fig.~1e). The integrated system comprises 44 MLX90393 nodes with
\dataneeded{number of effective/active nodes} active channels covering
\dataneeded{palmar coverage area, cm$^2$}, at a total thickness of
\dataneeded{system thickness, mm} and a mass of \dataneeded{system mass, g}.
It streams a local $2\times2$ frame at \dataneeded{2x2 frame rate, Hz} and a
full 44-node frame at \dataneeded{full-hand frame rate, Hz}, sustaining a
dropped-frame rate of \dataneeded{dropped-frame \% over 10 min} over 10~min
of continuous acquisition at a power draw of \dataneeded{system power, mW}.

\subsection*{Geometry-dependent amplification and physical characterization of magnetic cilia}
% -> Fig. 2
We fabricated NdFeB--PDMS cilia by mixing, degassing, mold filling,
magnetic pre-alignment, curing and demolding, followed by final
magnetization (Fig.~2a,b; Methods); the effective magnetization, calibrated
against finite-element case studies, is $M\approx82$~kA\,m$^{-1}$. We
produced cilia across a geometry matrix of lengths $L=3,5,8$~mm and
diameters $d=0.5,0.8,1.0$~mm (nine combinations) at a pitch of
\dataneeded{measured pitch $s$, mm} (Fig.~2c); for each geometry we measured
\dataneeded{$n$ cilia measured per geometry, target $\geq 30$ from $\geq 3$
patches} cilia and report height, diameter and pitch as mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.
Magnetization direction has little effect on composite stiffness: tensile
moduli are $0.72\pm0.11$ (non-magnetic, $n=5$), $0.85\pm0.09$ ($x$, $n=2$),
$0.73\pm0.11$ ($y$, $n=4$) and $0.68\pm0.11$~MPa ($z$-magnetized, $n=5$;
mean\,$\pm$\,s.d.). The $x$-magnetized group, whose magnetization is aligned
with the tensile axis, shows a trend toward higher stiffness, while the
remaining groups are indistinguishable within one standard deviation.
Composition, by contrast, sets the mechanical envelope: across the
Ecoflex~00-30 series, raising the NdFeB content from 50 to 70~wt\% leaves
the modulus nearly unchanged ($0.130\pm0.012$ to $0.152\pm0.026$~MPa),
whereas 80~wt\% stiffens the composite roughly sixfold
($0.836\pm0.095$~MPa) and reduces the elongation at break from above 400\%
to $\approx$150\%. These bounds define the compliance--magnetic-loading
trade-off available for cilia design, and the device composition (60~wt\%)
sits in the compliant regime while maximizing magnetic loading.
Loading--unloading hysteresis is \dataneeded{mechanical hysteresis, \%}
(Fig.~2d).

Sensitivity depends systematically on cilium geometry. We loaded each of
the nine geometries normally and tangentially at indentation depths of 1, 2
and 3~mm against an ATI force/torque reference and extracted the plateau
ratio $S=|\Delta\mathbf{B}|/F$ for each trial ($\geq 3$ trials per point;
44 valid condition points, one excluded artifact). A second-order response
surface over $(L,d)$ captures the trend with $R^2=0.92$ for tangential and
$R^2=0.68$ for normal loading, and sensitivities span approximately
3--50~mT\,N$^{-1}$ (Fig.~2e). The longest and thickest cilia ($L=8$~mm,
$d=1.0$~mm) give the largest median response (tangential
45~mT\,N$^{-1}$), consistent with the larger magnetic moment per cilium,
while large-depth points fall systematically below the small-signal trend,
consistent with deflection saturation. Repeated manual loading on a fixed
patch yields a peak-amplitude coefficient of variation of 13.4\% across 20
trials, dominated by the depth variability of the manual protocol. The
matched controls---a flat magnetic film, short magnetic pillars,
non-magnetic cilia and a bare MLX90393---required for the cilia gain
$G_{\mathrm{cilia}}=S_{\mathrm{cilia}}/S_{\mathrm{flat}}$ and the limit of
detection $\mathrm{LOD}=3\sigma_B/S$ remain to be acquired:
\dataneeded{flat-film / pillar / non-magnetic / bare-sensor control data,
$G_{\mathrm{cilia}}$ with 95\% CI, LOD}. The cilia respond rapidly and
withstand extended cycling: high-rate tap events give a 10--90\% response
time of 25~ms and a recovery of 26~ms for the fastest events (median
response 50~ms across 81 events), and cyclic fatigue over 150{,}000
compression cycles on three independent samples shows no significant
sensitivity degradation (within $\pm$4\% from 50k to 150k cycles on the two
samples with reliable acquisition); baseline drift over
\dataneeded{drift over $\geq$30~min} remains to be quantified (Fig.~2f).

\noindent\textit{Claim gate.} The term ``cilia-amplified'' is used only if the
cilia group significantly exceeds the flat control with a defined gain across
$\geq 3$ independent devices and statistical significance, after controlling for
total magnetic material and no-load field; otherwise we use ``cilia-enhanced''
or ``magnetic cilia tactile skin'' (see notes/claims\_table.md).
